Hands-on Exercise 3 - Spatio-Temporal Point Patterns Analysis

Overview

A spatio-temporal point process (space-time / spatial-temporal point process) is a random collection of points, where each point represents the time and location of an event.

  • E.g incidence of disease

  • E.g sightings or births of a species

  • E.g occurrences of fires, earthquakes, lightning strikes, tsunamis, volcanic eruptions.

The analysis of spatio-temporal point patterns is becoming increasingly necessary, due to the rapid emergence of geographically and temporally indexed data in a wide range of fields. Several spatio-temporal point patterns analysis methods have been introduced and implemented in R in the last ten years.

Getting Started

importing of data

The following data packages will be used. They are:

  • sf provides functions for importing processing and wrangling geospatial data,,

  • raster for handling raster data in R,

  • spatstat for performing Spatial Point Patterns Analysis such as kcross, Lcross, etc.,

  • sparr provides functions to estimate fixed and adaptive kernel-smoothed spatial relative risk surfaces via the density-ratio method and perform subsequent inference. Fixed-bandwidth spatiotemporal density and relative risk estimation is also supported

  • tmap provides functions to produce cartographic quality thematic maps, and

  • tidyverse, a family of R packages that provide functions to perform common data science tasks including and not limited to data import, data transformation, data wrangling and data visualisation.

pacman::p_load(sf, spatstat, sparr, 
               tmap, stpp, tidyverse)

Spatio-Temporal Point Pattern Analysis : stpp methods

It is also possible to use the stpp package to perform the Spatio-Temporal Point Pattern Analysis.

1. Preparing Spatio-Temporal Point Process Objecvt of stpp

The steps for preparation are as follows :

  1. Extracting forest fire coordinates from the fire point events
  2. Creating a data frame by combining the x- and y-coordinates and temporal event. Note that the temporal event must be in integer.
  3. Creating stpp spatio-temporal object
# 1. Extracting forest fire coordinates from the fire point events
coords <- st_coordinates(fire_sf)

#2. Creating a data frame by combining the x- and y-coordinates and temporal event.
fire_df <- data.frame(
  x = coords[, 1],  
  y = coords[, 2],
  t = fire_sf$`DayofYear`)

#3. Creating stpp spatio-temporal object
fire_stpp <- as.3dpoints(fire_df)

## This additional code is to ensure the output is in stpp spatio-temporal object class. Should expect to see output as stpp
class(fire_stpp)
[1] "stpp"

Now this plotted out to have a visual sense of our current data.

plot(fire_stpp)

2. Computing Spatio-temporal K function

 STIKhat() of stpp package is used to compute space-time inhomogeneous K-function, where output should be plotted to visualise output

kbb_stik <- STIKhat(fire_stpp)
plotK(kbb_stik)
Note

  • If the contours show high values for small values of uu and vv, it suggests clustering at short spatial and temporal distances, meaning events occur close to each other in both space and time.

  • If the contours are flat or show low values, it indicates a more random distribution or a lack of significant clustering at those distances.

The spatial and temporal extent of clustering can be understood by observing how rapidly the contours increase or decrease as we move along the axes. In the plot above, we can see clustering at specific distances by observing the spacing and values of the contour lines.

Analysis

Overview

  • The majority of the points are within the green to yellow range of the legend, showing that most fires in the region of Kepulauan Bangka Belitung happeed on the second half of the year (June to December ~Day 200-350). This is likely due to the Dry season of Indonesia, where the general rainfall amount drops due to the monsoon season.

  • Early days of the year (days 1-50) or the purple markers seemed to be rare and scattered. These early year fires (Jan and Feb) seem to be possibly be localized dry spells or human-caused ignition events instead due to its distribution of the data.

  • The fire season can also be denoted to increase progressively through the year rather than being a single sharp burst due to the gradual shift in gradient of colouring within our data points.

Spatial pattern

  • The fire points are NOT uniformly distributed!! With the clear clustering in the Northwest area, and the heavier clustering at the South area of the island. It is clear that the density of these areas are higher than that of the Northeast regions.

  • Therefore, it is very unlikely for there to be spatially independent! Since the points should be more evenly scattered across the whole landmass should it be spatially independent.
